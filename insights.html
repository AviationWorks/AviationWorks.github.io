Step 1 — The Raw Data

Total Trip, Reserve, Premium, and APU Trip counts by month. The APU launch month is highlighted.

Step 2 — Premium Rate Over Time

We calculate Premium Rate — Premium trips as a percentage of all trips that month. This strips out scheduling volume: if a month simply had fewer flights overall, all categories should shrink proportionally and the rate should hold steady. A falling rate means Premium shrank faster than the schedule.

Step 3 — Reserve Rate Over Time

We run the same analysis for Reserve trips. Reserve usage is our best proxy for operational stress — when the schedule is disrupted, Reserve climbs; when operations run smoothly, it falls. If Reserve also fell as a rate in months following APU launch, it tells us those months were genuinely less demanding and some Premium reduction is expected. The critical question is: by how much, and did Premium fall proportionally?

Step 4 — How Much Did Each Rate Really Change?

Each chart shows the composition of open trips for that month. Watch the Premium slice (green) relative to the Reserve slice (orange) — Reserve is our proxy for operational stress. If Premium shrinks faster than Reserve across the same period, volume alone cannot explain it.

Step 5 — Expected vs Actual: Volume Model

We now quantify the gap, month by month. The Volume Model asks: if each month had simply maintained the last pre-APU month's Premium rate, how many Premium trips would we expect? Expected = that month's total trips × the baseline Premium rate. The chart plots the expected and actual Premium lines across every month — the vertical gap between the two lines is the shortfall. This is the most straightforward baseline: it assumes every month is operationally identical to the baseline except for total trip count.

Step 6 — Expected vs Actual: Reserve-Normalised Model

The Volume Model doesn't account for genuine operational differences between months. The Reserve-Normalised Model gives each post-APU month credit for being operationally quieter (or busier): it scales the baseline Premium count by the same factor that Reserve changed in that month, then computes the remaining unexplained shortfall. This is the more conservative approach — it minimises the portion attributed to APU. The chart follows the same convention as Step 5: expected vs actual Premium trips by month.

Trip counts tell us how many events occurred. Pay hours tell us the financial magnitude. We first look at the raw change in Premium pay hours month over month, before any adjustment for operational stress.
